How to warm up before a run, a workout or a race: the RAMP structure, how long to spend by session, a drill
catalogue with cues, strides, stretching before running, and race-day timing. Post-run stretching and
recovery are in [stretching & recovery](/fitness/recovery-mobility/stretching-recovery/); pacing and
session types in [endurance](/fitness/training/endurance/); warm-up sets for lifting in
[strength training](/fitness/training/strength-training/).

## Why warm up

| Effect                        | Numbers                                                                       | Source                  |
| :---------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------- |
| muscle temperature rises      | 2.7–3.3 °C above rest after an active warm-up                                  | Raccuglia et al. 2016   |
| faster, stronger muscle       | short efforts improve 2–5 % per 1 °C of muscle temperature                     | Racinais & Oksa 2010    |
| performance overall           | warm-up improved performance in 79 % of criteria (32 high-quality studies)     | Fradkin et al. 2010     |
| oxygen uptake primed          | starting with raised baseline VO₂ helps efforts from ~10 s to over 5 min, if you start unfatigued | Bishop 2003 |
| heat is lost quickly          | muscle temperature falls exponentially within 15–30 min of stopping; ~1.3 °C in 30 min unheated | Raccuglia et al. 2016 |

### Injury prevention: what the evidence covers

| Programme or study                               | Result                                                                                 |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| FIFA 11+ (Soligard 2008), 1892 girls aged 13–17  | injury risk ratios: overall 0.68, severe 0.55, overuse 0.47 over one season             |
| FIFA 11+ meta-analysis (Thorborg 2017)           | 39 % fewer football injuries overall                                                   |
| warm-up, cool-down and stretching for runners (van Mechelen 1993), 326 men, 16 weeks | no effect: 5.5 vs 4.9 injuries per 1000 h (intervention vs control)       |

FIFA 11+ is a 20-min football programme of running, strength, plyometric and balance work, not a jog and
stretch; its benefit comes from that neuromuscular training. For recreational runners the injury case for
a warm-up is weak. Warm up to run better in hard sessions and races, and to feel better in the first
kilometres.

## The RAMP framework

Ian Jeffreys' structure (2007) for any warm-up: each phase leads into the next, and the last phase looks
like the session itself.

| Phase           | Goal                                                     | For running                                       | Time      |
| :-------------- | :------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ | :-------- |
| **R**aise       | heart rate, blood flow, muscle and core temperature      | easy jog, skipping, march                         | 5–15 min  |
| **A**ctivate    | switch on the muscles the session will load              | glute bridges, band walks, calf raises, pogo hops | 2–4 min   |
| **M**obilise    | take joints through the ranges the session needs         | leg swings, lunge with twist, hip circles, ankle rocks | 2–4 min |
| **P**otentiate  | raise intensity to or above session pace                 | A-skips, B-skips, bounds, strides, a short rep at race pace | 2–6 min |

Activate and mobilise are often combined into one circuit of drills. An easy run needs only the raise
phase; a race or interval session needs all four.

## How long to warm up

Longer and harder sessions need the warm-up less, and shorter and faster ones need it more.
Durations are coaching practice; the two trials in the notes back high-intensity efforts in the warm-up for
middle distance.

| Session                         | Warm-up                                                                     | Total       |
| :------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------- |
| easy or recovery run            | walk 1–2 min, then run the first 5–10 min slower than easy pace              | 0–5 min     |
| long run                        | as easy run; optional leg swings                                             | 0–5 min     |
| tempo or threshold              | 10–15 min easy, drills, 3–4 strides                                          | 15–20 min   |
| intervals or track              | 10–15 min easy, drills, 4–6 strides, 1–2 short reps at session pace          | 20–30 min   |
| 5 k or 10 k race                | 10–15 min easy, drills, 4–6 strides, 1–2 × 30–60 s at race pace              | 20–30 min   |
| half marathon                   | 10 min easy, 2–4 strides                                                     | 10–15 min   |
| marathon                        | 5–10 min walk and jog, 1–3 relaxed strides, or none; the first km is the rest of it | 0–10 min |

Notes:
- The marathon warm-up is short to save glycogen: race pace is sustainable from the gun, and the extra
  fuel cost of a long jog outweighs any gain.
- In a 5000 m trial, a warm-up with 3 × 250 m at 100 % effort beat the same warm-up at 70 % by 6.4 s
  (~0.5 %; Alves et al. 2023). An 800 m trial found the same with a 200 m run at race pace (Ingham et
  al. 2013).

## Drill catalogue

Do drills on a flat 20–30 m stretch, walking or jogging back as recovery. Reps are coaching practice, not
from trials. Quality over quantity: stop a drill when form breaks.

| Drill                   | What it trains                                    | Dose              | Key cue                                                     | Common fault                                   |
| :---------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ | :---------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| march (A-march)         | posture, knee lift, foot placement under the hip  | 2 × 20 m          | tall, knee to hip height, toes up, step down under the hip  | leaning back, reaching the foot forward        |
| A-skip                  | rhythm, knee drive, active ground contact         | 2–3 × 20 m        | skip with a high knee, punch the foot down under the hip    | loud heel strike, slow and floaty skip         |
| B-skip                  | A-skip plus pawing the leg back: hamstring action | 2 × 20 m          | lift, extend the lower leg, sweep it down and back          | kicking out straight and landing ahead of the body |
| high knees              | cadence, hip flexor drive                         | 2 × 15–20 m       | quick, short contacts on the balls of the feet; stay tall   | sitting back, arms flailing                    |
| butt kicks              | quick heel recovery, quad and knee range          | 2 × 15–20 m       | heel to the backside under the hip, not behind              | knee drifting back, slow cadence               |
| carioca (grapevine)     | hip rotation, lateral movement, coordination      | 2 × 20 m each way | shoulders square, hips rotate, light feet                   | shoulders twisting with the hips, crossing feet slowly |
| straight-leg bounds     | hamstrings, glutes, stiff springy contact         | 2 × 20 m          | nearly straight legs, pull the ground back under you        | bending the knees, bouncing on the heels       |
| pogo hops               | ankle and Achilles stiffness, elastic return      | 2 × 10–20 hops    | stiff ankles, quick contacts, heels just off the ground     | bending knees to jump, sinking into the heels  |
| skips for height        | power, triple extension                           | 2 × 20 m          | drive the knee and opposite arm up, push tall off the floor | going forward instead of up                    |
| skips for distance      | horizontal power                                  | 2 × 20 m          | long, powerful push; land under the hip                     | overreaching and braking on landing            |
| leg swings, front-back  | hip flexor and hamstring mobility                 | 10–15 per leg     | hold a wall, swing from the hip, trunk still                | arching the lower back to fake range           |
| leg swings, side-side   | adductor and abductor mobility                    | 10–15 per leg     | face the wall, swing across the body and out                | rotating the pelvis                            |
| walking lunge with twist| hip flexor length, thoracic rotation, balance     | 5–6 per side      | long step, rotate the trunk over the front leg              | front knee collapsing inward                   |
| hip circles (open-close gate) | hip rotation range                          | 5–8 each way per leg | knee up, circle it out and back; stand tall             | leaning the trunk to cheat range               |
| ankle rocks             | ankle dorsiflexion (calf and Achilles)            | 10–15 per side    | half-kneel or stand, drive the knee over the toes, heel down | heel lifting off the floor                     |
| strides                 | speed, relaxed form at fast pace, potentiation    | 4–6 × 15–20 s     | build over 5–10 s to ~85–90 %, hold, then ease off          | sprinting all-out, short recovery              |

### A-skips and B-skips

The A-skip is a skipping march: one high knee per skip, foot punched down under the hip. The B-skip adds
an extension of the lower leg at the top and a pulling "paw" back to the ground. Learn A before B; if the
B-skip turns into a kick forward, go back to A-skips.

Watch for the foot landing under the hip, not ahead of it, and the rhythm of the skip.

### Strides

Short relaxed accelerations: the potentiate phase of most warm-ups, and speed work after easy runs.

1. Flat 80–100 m, grass or track.
2. Accelerate smoothly over 5–10 s to about 85–90 % of top speed; hold 5–10 s; ease off. 15–20 s in all.
3. Walk back or stand: full recovery, 45–90 s, so every stride is fast and relaxed.
4. 4–6 reps. Cues: tall, relaxed face and hands, quick feet, arms drive back.

Watch for the gradual build-up and how relaxed the runners stay at speed.

## Stretching before running

Behm et al. (2016) pooled studies that tested performance 3–5 min after stretching:

| Stretch type                      | Change in performance straight after | Notes                                                                    |
| :-------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| static, under 60 s per muscle     | −1.1 %                               | trivial, and smaller still if dynamic activity follows                    |
| static, ≥ 60 s per muscle         | −4.6 %                               | avoid long holds right before a race                                      |
| static, all durations             | −3.7 %                               | bigger drop in strength (−4.8 %) than speed and power tests (−1.3 %)      |
| PNF                               | −4.4 %                               | do it well before (over 10 min) or after the session                      |
| dynamic                           | +1.3 %                               | best close to the start                                                   |

- All types raise range of motion for under ~30 min.
- In sprint-type sports, pre-exercise stretching was linked with ~54 % fewer acute muscle injuries (6
  studies); in endurance running there was no effect on overuse injuries.
- For runners: short static holds (under 60 s per muscle) for a tight spot are fine if drills and
  strides come after them. Otherwise favour dynamic drills.

## Race-day timing

| Minutes before the start | What to do                                                                          |
| :----------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 45–30                    | last toilet stop; start the easy jog (5 k and 10 k)                                  |
| 30–20                    | drills and strides                                                                   |
| 20–15                    | race-pace efforts, if used; finish the main warm-up 10–15 min before the start       |
| 15–0                     | keep warm and moving: layers or a throwaway top on, walk, light skips; 1–2 strides if the start pen allows |

- Muscle temperature falls within 15–30 min of stopping (Raccuglia 2016); insulating layers slow the
  loss. The 5000 m trial above left ~18 min between warm-up and start, with drills in the gap.
- Too hard or too close to the start fatigues you (Bishop 2003): keep race-pace bits short (30–60 s)
  and fully recovered.
- Big races with long start pens: warm up, keep layers on, and do leg swings and short jogs in the pen.

## Weather adjustments

| Condition                | Adjust                                                                                   |
| :----------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| cold (rough guide: under ~5 °C) | longer raise phase; keep tights, hat and gloves on until the last minutes; warm up indoors if possible; shorter gap to the start |
| cold and wet or windy    | throwaway top or bin bag in the start pen; drills under cover                             |
| hot (rough guide: over ~20 °C) or humid | shorter, easier warm-up in the shade: keep the drills and 2–4 strides, cut the jog; start well hydrated; a cooling vest or cold towel during the warm-up |
| hot, long race           | minimal warm-up; start conservatively; see [endurance](/fitness/training/endurance/) for heat pacing |

Cooling garments lower skin temperature without cooling the muscles, so they can be worn during a warm-up;
cold-water leg immersion cools muscle too, so re-warm afterwards (Racinais et al. 2015 heat consensus).
Heat illness signs (confusion, stopping sweating, collapse) need urgent medical help.

## Drills as technique work

- The same drills done as a stand-alone session (2 × a week, 10–20 min after an easy run) rehearse
  posture, foot placement and rhythm at low fatigue.
- Evidence is limited: drills clearly build coordination and elastic stiffness (pogo hops, bounds), but
  proof that they change distance-running form or economy is thin (Moore 2016: be cautious about
  prescribing one "economical" technique).
- Use them for: coordination, lower-leg stiffness, and a cue to carry into the run ("tall", "feet under
  hips"). Don't force a new foot strike.
- Progress: marches → skips → bounds; walking → skipping → running pace. Stop when form breaks.

## Recipes

### 10-minute easy-run warm-up
For easy and long runs, when the first kilometres feel stiff.

```text
2 min   brisk walk
2 min   leg swings, front-back and side-side (10 each leg), 8 hip circles each way
1 min   ankle rocks (10 per side), 10 pogo hops
5 min   start the run 30-60 s/km slower than easy pace, build to easy
```

### 20-minute workout or 5 k / 10 k race warm-up
For intervals, tempo runs and short races. Finish 10–15 min before a race start.

```text
10-12 min  easy jog, last 2 min at steady pace
 4 min     A-march, A-skip, B-skip, high knees, butt kicks (1 x 20 m each)
           leg swings (10 each way per leg), walking lunge with twist (5 per side)
 4 min     4 strides x 15-20 s, build to ~90 %, walk back
 2 min     race only: 1-2 x 30-60 s at race pace, 60-90 s easy between
```

### Marathon warm-up (minimal)
For the marathon, to save glycogen; the first 1–2 km at goal pace or slightly slower finish the job.

```text
-40 min   5 min easy walk, 5 min very easy jog
-30 min   leg swings (10 each way per leg), hip circles, ankle rocks
-25 min   1-3 relaxed strides (optional)
 0-15 min into the pen: keep a throwaway top on, walk, shake out
```

### Track session warm-up
For VO₂ max reps, 200–400 m repeats and hill sprints.

```text
12-15 min  easy jog, last 3 min steady
 3 min     leg swings, walking lunges with twist, hip circles
 6 min     march, A-skip, B-skip, carioca, straight-leg bounds, pogo hops
           (2 x 20 m each, walk back)
 5 min     5 strides x 20 s, build to 90 %; last one at session pace
 2 min     rest, then start the first rep
```

### 5-minute "desk to door" warm-up
For a lunch-hour or after-work run after hours of sitting: undoes stiff hips and calves.

1. 20 bodyweight squats or sit-to-stands, slowly (30 s).
2. Walking lunge with twist, 5 per side (1 min).
3. Leg swings front-back and side-side, 10 each per leg (1 min).
4. Ankle rocks, 10 per side; 20 calf raises (1 min).
5. March on the spot, then 30 s high knees and 30 s butt kicks (1.5 min).
6. Start the run gently for the first 5 min.

### Drills-only technique session
For 1–2 sessions a week, after an easy run on grass or a track, 15–20 min in all.

| Block        | Drills                                                     | Dose                        |
| :----------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| coordination | A-march, A-skip, B-skip                                    | 2 × 20 m each, walk back    |
| elastic      | pogo hops (both legs, then single leg)                     | 2 × 15 hops each            |
| power        | skips for height, skips for distance, straight-leg bounds  | 2 × 20 m each, full walk back |
| lateral      | carioca both ways                                          | 2 × 20 m each               |
| transfer     | strides, carrying the drill cue ("feet under hips")        | 4 × 20 s                    |

Add hops and bounds gradually if new to plyometrics, and not the day before a hard session.
