Making images and videos in **Blender 5.x** (5.2 LTS): EEVEE vs Cycles vs Workbench, viewport shading and
matcaps, world and HDRI lighting, lights and their units, three-point lighting and light linking, cameras,
render settings and denoising, color management (AgX), image and video output, website turntables and
transparent PNGs, and speed on a Mac. Why light and materials behave as they do is in
[Materials & lighting](/3d/fundamentals/materials-lighting/); materials themselves are in
[Materials, UVs & texturing](/3d/blender/materials-uv/). Keys are Windows-style; on macOS read Ctrl as ⌘ (or
⌃), Alt as ⌥, and press fn with F-keys unless macOS is set to use them as standard function keys.

## Render engines

Properties > **Render** tab > Render Engine.

| | EEVEE | Cycles | Workbench |
| :- | :---- | :----- | :-------- |
| Method | real-time rasterizer (like a game engine) with optional screen-space ray tracing | path tracer: simulates light bounces | flat preview shading |
| Speed per frame | fractions of a second to seconds | seconds to minutes | instant |
| Global illumination | light probes and screen-trace (Render > Raytracing, off by default) | physically correct bounces | none |
| Shadows, reflections, glass | good; screen-space limits (off-screen objects don't reflect) | accurate | basic |
| Noise | none (anti-aliasing samples: 64 render, 16 viewport by default) | yes: needs samples + denoising | none |
| Baking | no | yes ([baking](/3d/blender/materials-uv/)) | no |
| Use for | animation, stylized work, previews, look close to real-time engines | final stills, product shots, glass and caustics, bakes | modeling previews, playblasts, clay renders |

EEVEE is the default engine for new files. Material Preview shading uses EEVEE, so it predicts what EEVEE
renders.

## Viewport shading & matcaps

**Z** opens the shading pie; Shift+Z toggles wireframe; Alt+Z X-ray. The header's four spheres do the same,
and the ▾ next to them holds options.

| Mode | Engine | Use | Options worth knowing |
| :--- | :----- | :-- | :-------------------- |
| Wireframe | overlay | topology, hidden parts | X-ray opacity |
| Solid | Workbench | modeling and sculpting | Lighting Studio / **MatCap** / Flat; Color Material / Object / Random / Attribute / Texture; Cavity; Backface Culling; Shadow |
| Material Preview | EEVEE | material work under a studio HDRI | pick an HDRI and rotate it; toggle Scene Lights / Scene World |
| Rendered | the scene's engine | lighting, final look | Cycles viewport samples (1024 max by default); turn on viewport Denoise; use a render region |

**MatCaps** (Solid > Lighting > MatCap) are shiny "material capture" spheres: they exaggerate every bump and
pinch, so they are the fastest way to check surface quality while modeling. Color **Random** tells objects
apart; Color **Texture** shows image textures in Solid mode without the cost of Material Preview.

## World & HDRI lighting

Properties > **World** sets the background and ambient light (a **Background** shader: Color + Strength).
An **HDRI** (high dynamic range 360° photo, `.hdr` or `.exr`) lights a scene realistically in one step.

1. World tab > Surface > Color: click the socket dot > **Environment Texture**; Open an HDRI (Poly Haven,
   ambientCG: CC0).
2. **Strength** scales it (1 = as shot).
3. Rotate: Shader Editor, switch to **World**, select the Environment Texture node, **Ctrl+T** (Node
   Wrangler) adds Mapping; change Rotation Z.
4. Hide the background but keep its light: Render > Film > **Transparent** (then composite over any
   color), or in Cycles World > Ray Visibility > uncheck Camera.

Use the Environment Texture node, not Image Texture: it maps equirectangular (lat-long) images correctly.
EEVEE treats world light as indirect and stores it in an internal light probe, so it is less precise than in
Cycles.

## Lights

Shift+A > **Light**. Select a light, then Properties > Object Data (green bulb) for its settings. Right-click
> Adjust Light Power changes it interactively.

| Light | Unit | Size control | Like |
| :---- | :--- | :----------- | :--- |
| Point | Power, W | Radius (bigger = softer shadows) | bulb, candle |
| Spot | Power, W | Radius; Beam Shape Angle and Blend (soft edge) | stage light, flashlight |
| Area | Power, W | Shape Square / Rectangle / Disk / Ellipse, Size | softbox, window, screen |
| Sun | Strength, W/m² | Angle (angular diameter; bigger = softer) | sunlight; position doesn't matter, only rotation |

Watts here are radiant power, not a bulb's electrical rating: the manual lists an 800 lm LED bulb as about 2.1
W and clear-sky sun as about 1000 W/m², but rendering those needs camera-like exposure. New point, spot and
area lights start at 10 W, a new Sun at 1, and the startup scene's light at 1000 W. In practice: put the
light at its real distance, raise power until the subject reads well, then fine-tune with **Exposure** in
Color Management. Every light also has Color, **Temperature** (Kelvin) and an Exposure slider.

### Three-point lighting

The classic product and character setup (theory and diagram in
[Materials & lighting](/3d/fundamentals/materials-lighting/)):

1. **Key**: an Area light 45° to one side of the camera and 30–45° above the subject, aimed at it (rotate it,
   or add a **Track To** constraint targeting the subject). The brightest light; its size sets shadow
   softness.
2. **Fill**: a larger Area light on the opposite side at about ⅓–½ of the key's power, to open up shadows.
3. **Rim** (back light): behind and above the subject, facing the camera, strong and narrow, to outline the
   silhouette against the background.
4. Optional: a dim HDRI or a gray World color (Strength 0.1–0.3) so shadows are never pure black.

### Light linking

Light linking makes a light affect only some objects; shadow linking picks which objects block it. Works in
Cycles and EEVEE (emissive meshes: Cycles only).

1. Select the light, Properties > **Object** > **Shading** > Light Linking.
2. New collection, drag objects or collections from the Outliner into it; set each to Include or Exclude.
3. Shortcut: select the receivers, then the light last, Ctrl+L > Link Receivers to Emitter.

Use it for a rim light on a character only, or to keep a key light off the backdrop.

## Camera

| Task | How |
| :--- | :-- |
| Look through the active camera | numpad 0 (view pie > View Camera) |
| Make an object the active camera | select it, Ctrl + numpad 0 (View > Cameras > Set Active Object as Camera) |
| Put the camera where you are looking | Ctrl + Alt + numpad 0 (View > Align View > Align Active Camera to View) |
| Fly the camera with the viewport | N sidebar > View > View Lock > **Camera to View** (red dashed frame), then navigate |
| Focal length | Camera Properties > Lens > Focal Length (50 mm default; 85–135 mm flattering products and faces; 18–24 mm wide interiors) |
| Orthographic | Lens > Type Orthographic, Orthographic Scale (icons, isometric shots) |
| Depth of field | Camera Properties > **Depth of Field** on; Focus on Object (pick the subject) or Distance; **F-Stop** (2.8 default; lower = blurrier) |
| Clipping | Lens > Clip Start / End (objects outside are cut) |
| Composition | Viewport Display > Composition Guides (Thirds, Center, Golden…); Passepartout darkens outside the frame |
| Aim at a subject | Object Constraints > **Track To** (target an Empty or the subject) |
| Resolution / aspect | Output tab > Format (1920 × 1080, 1080 × 1080 for social, 1080 × 1920 vertical) |

## Render settings

**Cycles** (Render tab):

| Setting | Default | Guidance |
| :------ | :------ | :------- |
| Device | CPU | **GPU Compute** after enabling a device in Preferences > System > Cycles Render Devices (Metal on Apple Silicon) |
| Sampling > Render > Noise Threshold | 0.01 | 0.01–0.05; adaptive sampling stops clean pixels early |
| Max Samples | 4096 | 256–1024 is plenty with denoising for most stills |
| Time Limit | 0 (off) | cap per frame for previews |
| Denoise (Render / Viewport) | on, Automatic → OpenImageDenoise | OIDN everywhere; OptiX only on NVIDIA; Passes Albedo + Normal keeps detail |
| Light Paths > Max Bounces | 12 total | lower (4–6) for speed; raise Transmission for lots of glass |
| Light Paths > Clamping > Indirect Light | 10 | lower (1–5) if fireflies (bright speckles) remain; 0 disables |
| Film > Transparent | off | on for PNG cut-outs; add Transparent Glass for see-through glass |
| Performance > Persistent Data | off | on for animations: keeps scene data between frames |

**EEVEE** (Render tab):

| Setting | Default | Guidance |
| :------ | :------ | :------- |
| Sampling > Render / Viewport samples | 64 / 16 | 64–128 renders; more smooths soft shadows and depth of field |
| Raytracing | off | on for reflections and GI; Method Screen-Trace, or Light Probe with placed probes |
| Shadows, Volumes, Motion Blur, Depth of Field | panels | DoF follows the camera settings |
| Light probes | Shift+A > Light Probe: Sphere, Plane, Volume | local reflections, mirrors and indirect light when ray tracing is off |
| Film > Transparent, Filter Size | off, 1.5 px | as in Cycles |

**Output tab > Format**: Resolution X / Y and **%** (render at 50% for tests), Frame Rate; **Render Region**
(Ctrl+B in camera view draws it, Ctrl+Alt+B clears) renders only part of the frame.

| Action | Keys |
| :----- | :--- |
| Render the current frame | F12 |
| Render the animation | Ctrl+F12 |
| Show the render window / play back rendered frames | F11 / Ctrl+F11 |
| Cancel | Esc |
| Save the render | Image > Save As in the render window (still renders are not saved automatically) |
| Quick preview of the viewport | View > Render Viewport Preview / Render Playblast (Workbench or EEVEE only) |

## Color management

Render tab > **Color Management** converts the linear render into display colors. Same settings apply to
the viewport, the render window and saved PNG/JPEG/WebP (EXR stays linear).

| Setting | Default | Options and use |
| :------ | :------ | :-------------- |
| Display | sRGB | Display P3 for wide-gamut Mac screens (pair with a wide working space) |
| View (transform) | **AgX** | AgX: photoreal, handles bright light gracefully; **Standard**: no tone mapping, flat colors match hex values (UI, stylized, compositing); **Khronos PBR Neutral**: base colors stay faithful (product shots); ACES 1.3 / 2.0; Filmic (deprecated); False Color (exposure heat map) |
| Look | None | AgX looks: Punchy, Base Contrast, Low / Medium / High Contrast, plus a black-and-white look |
| Exposure | 0 | in stops: +1 doubles brightness |
| Gamma | 1 | leave at 1 |
| White Balance | off | Temperature / Tint, or pick a white point |
| Working Space (5.0+) | Linear Rec.709 | keep for glTF / web work |

Matching the web: three.js renders with no tone mapping unless you set one; it has `AgXToneMapping` and
`NeutralToneMapping` (Khronos PBR Neutral). Pick the same transform on both sides when you compare
Blender renders with the live model ([Three.js lights & shadows](/typescript/three-js/lights-shadows/)).

## Output: images & video

Output tab > **Output**: File Path (`//` = next to the .blend; `//render/frame_` gives `frame_0001.png`),
**Media Type** Image / Multi-Layer EXR / Video, then format settings.

| Format | Alpha | Bits | Use |
| :----- | :---- | :--- | :-- |
| PNG | RGBA | 8 / 16 | lossless stills and image sequences; transparent cut-outs |
| JPEG | no | 8 | small previews |
| WebP | RGBA | 8 | web-ready stills (quality slider or lossless) |
| AVIF | RGBA | 8 / 10 / 12 | smaller web stills; HDR-capable |
| OpenEXR / Multi-Layer EXR | yes | 16 / 32 float | linear data for compositing; render passes in one file |
| Video (Media Type Video) | codec-dependent | 8–12 | Encoding panel: container MPEG-4 + H.264 or AV1; WebM + VP9 (supports alpha); QuickTime + ProRes for editing |

**Render animations as image sequences**, then encode: a crash loses one frame instead of the video, you can
re-render single frames, and you can encode several formats from one render. Enable **Overwrite** off +
**Placeholders** on to resume or split a render across machines.

```sh
# macOS: brew install ffmpeg
ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i frame_%04d.png \
  -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -crf 18 turntable.mp4

# transparent video for the web (VP9 WebM with alpha)
ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i frame_%04d.png \
  -c:v libvpx-vp9 -pix_fmt yuva420p -b:v 0 -crf 30 \
  turntable.webm
```

`yuv420p` needs even width and height. Blender can also encode directly (Media Type Video) or assemble a
sequence in the Video Sequencer.

## Rendering for websites

| Shot | Setup |
| :--- | :---- |
| Transparent PNG / WebP cut-out | Film > Transparent; PNG or WebP, **RGBA**; shadow on an invisible floor: floor object > Object Properties > Visibility > Mask > **Shadow Catcher** (Cycles) |
| Studio product shot | seamless backdrop (a plane with one edge extruded up and beveled), three area lights or an HDRI, 85–100 mm lens, Khronos PBR Neutral or AgX |
| Turntable video | camera parented to an Empty that rotates 360° (recipe below); 120–240 frames at 30 fps |
| Hero image | 2× the displayed size (e.g. 2400 px for a 1200 px slot) for Retina, saved as WebP or AVIF |
| Poster frame for a 3D viewer | same camera angle as the web viewer's default, so the swap from image to live model is seamless |

## Performance on a Mac

- Preferences > System > Cycles Render Devices > **Metal**, tick the Apple GPU; Render tab > Device **GPU
  Compute**. Apple Silicon also gets GPU denoising and hardware ray tracing (MetalRT, Auto by default).
  Intel Macs are no longer supported by Blender 5.
- Cycles: Noise Threshold 0.02–0.05 with **Max Samples** 256–512 and OIDN denoise; Light Paths bounces 4–6;
  keep indirect clamping on.
- Test at 25–50% resolution and with a **Render Region**; use EEVEE for look development and animations.
- Render tab > **Simplify**: cap Max Subdivision and Texture Limit for previews.
- Animations: Persistent Data on; render image sequences; keep the Mac plugged in (battery mode throttles
  the GPU).
- Unified memory is shared with the system: close heavy apps, and downsize 8K textures to 2K–4K.

## Does this survive export?

Lights, cameras, render settings and color management mostly stay in Blender.

| Blender thing | glTF / engines | Notes |
| :------------ | :------------- | :---- |
| Point, Spot, Sun lights | `KHR_lights_punctual` if Include > **Punctual Lights** is on | intensities are converted (Lighting Mode Standard: physical units); expect to re-tune in the engine |
| Area lights | not exported | fake with an emissive plane or bake the light |
| World / HDRI | not exported | load the same HDRI as the environment in three.js (`HDRLoader`) or Godot (`WorldEnvironment`) |
| Cameras | exported if Include > **Cameras** is on | perspective and orthographic |
| Cycles lighting and shadows | only via **baking** (Combined or Diffuse lighting into textures or lightmaps) | static scenes for the web: bake, then light cheaply in the engine |
| AgX / color management | not in the file | set tone mapping in the engine to match |
| Rendered stills and videos | ship as PNG / WebP / AVIF / MP4 / WebM | poster images and fallbacks for web 3D viewers |

Per target: [Web / Three.js](/3d/export/web-threejs/), [Godot](/3d/export/godot/),
[GTA V](/3d/export/gta-v/) (GTA lights are placed in-game or via map files, not from Blender renders).

## Recipes

### Studio turntable render
Use for a product or model preview video on a website.

1. Model at the world origin, origin at the bottom center.
2. Shift+A > Empty > Plain Axes at (0, 0, 0). Select the camera, then the Empty, Ctrl+P > Object: the camera
   now orbits with the Empty.
3. Frame 1: Empty Rotation Z = 0, hover the field, **I**. Frame 121: Rotation Z = 360, I.
4. Frame range 1–120 (so frame 121, identical to frame 1, is never rendered). In the Timeline or Graph Editor:
   select all keys, T (Interpolation) > **Linear** for constant speed.
5. Lighting: three area lights parented to nothing (the model turns through fixed light) or parented to the
   Empty (light turns with the camera).
6. Output: `//render/frame_`, PNG RGBA, Film Transparent if the page supplies the background; Ctrl+F12.
7. Encode with the FFmpeg commands above.

### Transparent PNG product shot
Use for cut-out images on a landing page.

1. Cycles; Film > **Transparent**.
2. Add a floor plane under the product; Object Properties > Visibility > Mask > **Shadow Catcher**: only the
   shadow renders.
3. Key + fill area lights; 85 mm lens; View Transform Khronos PBR Neutral.
4. Output PNG or WebP, **RGBA**; F12; Image > Save As.

### Quick realistic lighting with an HDRI
Use to preview a model or light an exterior in one step.

1. Download a 2K–4K HDRI (Poly Haven), World > Color > Environment Texture > Open.
2. Ctrl+T (Node Wrangler) on it; rotate Mapping Z until the sun direction flatters the model.
3. Add a Sun with the same direction for crisp shadows (the HDRI's sun is often soft in EEVEE).
4. Film > Transparent if the background shouldn't show.

### Fast, clean Cycles still on a MacBook
Use for a portfolio render in minutes, not hours.

1. Device GPU Compute (Metal).
2. Noise Threshold 0.03, Max Samples 512, Denoise OIDN with Albedo + Normal.
3. Light Paths: Max Bounces 6; lower Clamping > Indirect Light if fireflies appear.
4. Render at 50% to check; 100% for the final; save as PNG or EXR.

### Light a character with three points
Use for character turnarounds and thumbnails.

1. Key: Area light, Size 1 m, 45° left and 40° up, Track To the head; 100–300 W at 2–3 m, then adjust.
2. Fill: Area 2 m, opposite side, about a third of the key.
3. Rim: Spot or narrow Area behind, above, aimed at the shoulders; enable Light Linking to the character only
   so it doesn't hit the backdrop.
4. World: gray, Strength 0.2. Check with View Transform AgX and False Color for clipped highlights.

### Encode a PNG sequence to MP4 and WebM
Use after any animation render.

1. Render frames to `//render/frame_` (PNG, RGBA if transparency is needed).
2. Run the FFmpeg commands from the output section in that folder.
3. For the web: MP4 H.264 for opaque video, WebM VP9 with alpha for transparent video; use a PNG
   poster frame.
